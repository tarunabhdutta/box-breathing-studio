Box breathing: how to do it, with a free guided timer

Box breathing, also called square breathing or Sama Vritti in yoga, is a slow, even breathing pattern with four equal sides: breathe in, hold, breathe out, hold. It is widely used to calm the body before stressful moments and to settle focus. Box Breathing Studio counts every side for you with a calm voice, so you can close your eyes.

How to do Box breathing, step by step

  1. Sit upright and relax your shoulders. Breathe through your nose.
  2. Inhale slowly for a count of 4.
  3. Hold the breath in for 4.
  4. Exhale smoothly for 4.
  5. Hold with the lungs empty for 4.
  6. Repeat for about 10 rounds, or 4 to 5 minutes.

Box breathing in Box Breathing Studio

The app offers three levels: Beginner (3-3-3-3), Intermediate (4-4-4-4) and Advanced (6-6-6-6), 10 rounds each, with voice cues and a soft tone on every phase. Use the Custom Builder for any other ratio from 1 to 12 seconds per side.

Why people practise Box breathing

  • A steady, slow rhythm gives the mind one simple thing to follow.
  • Slow breathing with long, even exhales is widely used to help the body shift toward a calmer state.
  • Equal counts make it easy to learn and easy to repeat anywhere: before a meeting, an exam or sleep.

Who should be careful

  • High blood pressure or heart conditions: skip the breath holds. Use Extended Exhale, or the Custom Builder with the hold set to 0.
  • Pregnancy: avoid holding the breath.
  • Dizziness or light-headedness: stop and breathe normally. Shorten the counts next time.
  • Breathing exercises support well-being. They are not a treatment for any medical or mental-health condition. Speak to a doctor if you have a health concern.

Frequently asked questions

What is box breathing?

A four-part breathing pattern with equal counts: inhale, hold, exhale, hold. It is also known as square breathing or Sama Vritti pranayama.

How long should I do box breathing?

Start with 4 to 5 minutes, about 10 rounds. Many people practise once or twice a day, or whenever they feel tense.

Is there a free box breathing app?

Yes. Box Breathing Studio is free, open source and works offline on Android, iPhone and Windows, with no ads or signup.

Is box breathing good for anxiety?

Many people use it to settle nerves in the moment. It is a self-help technique, not a replacement for professional care.
