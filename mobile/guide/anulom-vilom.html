Anulom Vilom and Nadi Shodhana: alternate nostril breathing

Anulom Vilom, closely related to Nadi Shodhana pranayama, is alternate nostril breathing: you breathe in through one nostril and out through the other, then switch. It is one of the best-known pranayama practices for balance and calm. The hardest part for beginners is remembering which side comes next, which is exactly what the app's voice guidance handles.

How to do Anulom Vilom, step by step

  1. Sit upright. Rest your left hand on your knee.
  2. Use your right hand: the thumb closes the right nostril, the ring finger (and little finger) closes the left nostril.
  3. Close the right nostril and inhale through the left.
  4. Close the left nostril, release the right, and exhale through the right.
  5. Inhale through the right, then close it and exhale through the left. That is one round.
  6. Continue for 5 to 10 rounds at a slow, even pace.

Anulom Vilom in Box Breathing Studio

Two routines: Nadi Shodhana guides two rounds of five cycles (left to right, then right to left) at 4-4-4-4, and Nadi Shodhana Box follows the classical 8-phase alternation with 4-second holds. The voice says "Inhale left", "Exhale right" and so on, so you never lose your place.

Why people practise Anulom Vilom

  • The steady alternation keeps attention gently occupied.
  • Slow nasal breathing with even counts helps many people feel balanced and calm.
  • It pairs well with meditation and can be done seated anywhere.

Who should be careful

  • High blood pressure or heart conditions: skip the breath holds. Use Extended Exhale, or the Custom Builder with the hold set to 0.
  • Pregnancy: avoid holding the breath.
  • Dizziness or light-headedness: stop and breathe normally. Shorten the counts next time.
  • Breathing exercises support well-being. They are not a treatment for any medical or mental-health condition. Speak to a doctor if you have a health concern.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Anulom Vilom and Nadi Shodhana?

Both are alternate nostril breathing. Many teachers use the names interchangeably; Nadi Shodhana is often taught with breath holds, while Anulom Vilom is often taught without.

Which nostril do you start with in Anulom Vilom?

It is usually started by inhaling through the left nostril, with the right nostril closed by the thumb.

How long should I do Anulom Vilom?

Start with 3 to 5 minutes and build up gradually. Skip the holds if you have high blood pressure.
