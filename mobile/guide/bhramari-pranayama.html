Bhramari pranayama: the humming bee breath

Bhramari takes its name from the Sanskrit word for the black Indian bee. You breathe in, then make a steady humming sound like a bee as you breathe out. The gentle vibration and the long exhale make it one of the most soothing pranayama practices, and it is easy for beginners.

How to do Bhramari pranayama, step by step

  1. Sit upright with your eyes closed and your face relaxed.
  2. Gently close your ears with your index fingers or thumbs (optional).
  3. Inhale slowly through your nose.
  4. Exhale slowly while humming 'mmm' with your lips closed, until the breath is complete.
  5. Feel the vibration in your head and face. Repeat 5 to 7 times.

Bhramari pranayama in Box Breathing Studio

The Bhramari routine guides 7 rounds of 4-second inhale and 8-second humming exhale, with a built-in hum tone you can follow. It is also part of the 15-minute Daily Sequence.

Why people practise Bhramari pranayama

  • The long humming exhale naturally slows the breath.
  • Focusing on sound and vibration draws attention away from racing thoughts.
  • It needs no breath holding, so most people can practise it comfortably.

Who should be careful

  • High blood pressure or heart conditions: skip the breath holds. Use Extended Exhale, or the Custom Builder with the hold set to 0.
  • Pregnancy: avoid holding the breath.
  • Dizziness or light-headedness: stop and breathe normally. Shorten the counts next time.
  • Breathing exercises support well-being. They are not a treatment for any medical or mental-health condition. Speak to a doctor if you have a health concern.

Frequently asked questions

What is Bhramari pranayama?

A yoga breathing practice where you exhale with a humming 'mmm' sound like a bee. It is also called the humming bee breath.

How many times should I do Bhramari?

Five to seven rounds is a common starting point. The app's guided routine runs 7 rounds.

When is the best time to practise Bhramari?

Any calm moment works. Many people practise it in the evening or before meditation.
